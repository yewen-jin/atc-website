01. GATEKEEPER

Do you accept cookies?

Do things change when you know someone is observing?

CAM_04 // GATEKEEPER ARCHIVE // 01

Gatekeeper is a video installation piece that was first shown in an autonomous artist takeover.

It was a politically charged time and the building had an institutional history. The space was buzzing, but there was also some paranoia in the air. Police eventually stationed themselves at the doors and cut off entry.

The audience were amused and also a little spooked by the CCTV cameras on this piece. Someone turned them away. I was told a couple of people felt pissed off about feeling watched.

The piece features a mysterious femme character luring you towards her with cookies. She has long white hair, a silver gown, silver tree branches wrapped around her shoulders, and a spiky silver headpiece that covers her eyes.

Make up your own mind about what she has to say. In my mind, she represents the ritual of entering an online space. I use the word 'ritual' intentionally but loosely here. Like the morning ritual of making a coffee. Or the ritual of signing a contract when starting a job.

The piece explores themes of data collection and tracking, where consent is often assumed.

When you click 'accept', you are often telling websites 'I consent to you collecting and selling information about me.' Your data is sometimes sold to the highest bidder and used to build profiles on who you are and what you like. This goes towards targeted adverts. I am not here to be a doomer about this, but I did want the piece to highlight that a click is not a neutral act.

The piece asks: What are cookies, and where do they go? I've been told it's not that deep, but I've remained curious.

Gatekeeper sought to make invisible tracking visible with the overt use of CCTV cameras on the installation.

// AI statement

The videos in this piece originally combined graphic design with performance, and also some cutaways of cookies which were made with generative AI. As a group, we since decided that we would not use generative AI in this project - and for that I am grateful. Future exhibits of this piece will not include any AI videos, and all the work you see on this web-site and online exhibition are human-made.

02. GOING UNDERGROUND

Does 'technology' need to be plugged in or battery powered?

CAM_04 // UNDERGROUND ARCHIVE // 02

Imagination is powerful. The team had been talking on the group chat about technology. I was having floods of dreams. Too much to describe here.

Conversations came through our group chat about what counts as 'technology'. Like others on the team, I was looking at old tech, 'low' tech, woke tech, blue tech.

What about no-tech? Could it be possible to make an interactive art piece that relies on the player's imagination?

I took these thoughts into other autonomous spaces and started drawing hand-activated 'portals' on walls. This period of development was raw and real.

I saw a daimon come from one of these portals. No I wasn't on drugs. She was painted by multiple hands that came in after me. The room thrummed with the feeling of being alive. It's like she knew she had permission to come out. She was feminine and fierce.

When I say 'daimon' I mean spirit. Not a bad spirit. But a powerful one. The work had started to take on an esoteric flavour. What if technology could house spirit? And what if it couldn't?

Memories of this time… sleeping on the dust-covered floor with people I didn't yet know. Collective noise-making with punks, rappers, electronic musicians, and other DIY artists. The determination to resist dystopia. Queer carnage. Yells of Viva Palestina. Boxing. Soundsystems. Ghosts from the past. Artworks shared via USB. Throwing dildos at billboard prints of billionaires. Play.

Most images of this work are lost or redacted. (Back up your phones ppl).

I guess you'll have to use your imagination.

03. GET OVER YOUR CRINGE

What do you want a better internet to look like?

CAM_04 // CRINGE ARCHIVE // 03

This piece started in response to our team chat. As a group we explored what questions we wanted to ask. I created a deck of 52 cards with questions inspired by these conversations.

The cards linked to a private Telegram group intended to evoke further discussion online. Although mostly quiet, at birth it was a bit unhinged. People changed the profile picture and name of the group. Started throwing in random images and talking nonsense. Again, more esoteric flavours with a dose of anarchy.

I've given away 74 of these question cards, and used them to have about 200 conversations across the UK, with folks of different ages, cultural backgrounds, and locations. My body is thankful that these interactions did not become my social media content. Being able to look people in the eyes as they spoke, my soul learnt so much.

For starters: Many people actually care about this stuff. And sometimes personal stories can get triggered by a choice of words. And also many people were anti-tech. My biased eyes experienced this as associating all tech with capitalist tech.

Since then, several of these questions have been performed in BSL and are getting ready for an interactive installation. Watch this space.

04. CHAPTER 0

How does AI influence you?

CAM_04 // CHAPTER 0 ARCHIVE // 04

Chapter 0 is a video art piece and interactive installation that follows an impressionable hacker as they drift into AI-induced psychosis, featuring a clingy chatbot that is as sickly sweet as the sticky glaze it serves.

This was an exercise in rapid-prototyping and nailing an immersive production process. It was shown and produced as artist in residence at the Immersive Experience Network Summit.

08. CHAPTER -88

What is on the other side?

CAM_04 // CHAPTER -88 ARCHIVE // 08

After a period of research-heavy work, Chapter -88 felt refreshingly intuitive. It started from daydreams without words and the 'this-doesn't-make-any-kind-of-rational-sense' side of the brain.

The piece shows a glassy-eyed androgyne with cyber sigil influenced facepaint, sat behind an empty fishtank and drifting into visions of an alternative world.

Chapter -88 was first shown at arebyte Digital Art Centre (Bring Your Own Beamer) and made alongside IN4 & The Creative Engine Skills bootcamp.

09. SERAPHIM

CAM_04 // SERAPHIM ARCHIVE // 09

Concept by Manaal Asim (link) - Manaal built on Blender. Creative discussions. Manaal built a cube Seraphim.

We spent time devising a concept, Manaal went and built, and I created video outputs on TouchDesigner, ready for interactive installations.

This work follows a concept set in the backrooms, where angels process code faster than any human. Biblically accurate angels in the BTS of the internet, being able to conceptualise code that would fill a whole room.

As humans, processing information one chunk at a time while scrolling. Seeing a tiny fragment of what is online at a time. Social media as a bombardment of energy. The interface - a tiny fragment of what's online.

A seraphim brought to life through a series of cubes on Blender, which I then took screen recordings into TouchDesigner and also dropped into Unreal Engine.

Creating art beyond the senses and beyond our comprehension. So much coming from so many places. Tailoring the feed because it would be so overwhelming otherwise.

The angel knows all, shows all, and will not do that in front of you. Like accepting the cookies, putting self into a massive database. The thing you can't comprehend. Even a little (Krsna true form).
